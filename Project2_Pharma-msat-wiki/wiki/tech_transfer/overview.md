## Definition

Tech transfer (technology transfer) is the structured process of moving a pharmaceutical manufacturing process from one site, scale, or organization to another — along with all of the knowledge, documentation, and expertise needed to reproduce it faithfully.

The phrase "tech transfer" is used loosely in the industry to describe several distinct activities:

1. **R&D to Clinical Manufacturing** — Moving a lab-scale process to a GMP clinical site for Phase I/II trials
2. **Clinical to Commercial** — Scaling up and transferring a process to a commercial manufacturing site ahead of launch
3. **Site-to-Site** — Moving a commercial process from one manufacturing facility to another (e.g., for supply chain resilience or cost optimization)
4. **External (Outsourcing)** — Transferring a process to a Contract Manufacturing Organization (CMO)

All four involve the same core challenge: the receiving site must be able to manufacture the product to the same specifications as the sending site, without the institutional knowledge that naturally accumulates at the originating site.

---

## Why It's Hard

Tech transfer failures are common and costly. The root causes are nearly always one of three things:

**1. Process knowledge gaps**  
The sending site knows more about the process than is captured in the documents. Tacit knowledge — the things operators and scientists "just know" — often doesn't make it into the transfer package. When the receiving site encounters an unexpected behavior, they don't have context to understand it.

**2. Scale and equipment differences**  
A process that works at 10L might not work the same way at 1000L. Heat transfer, mixing dynamics, shear forces, and mass transfer can all change in ways that affect product quality. Similarly, different equipment models — even of the same type — can behave differently.

**3. Inadequate process characterization**  
If the process parameters haven't been systematically studied to understand which ones matter and why, there's no way to know which differences between sites are acceptable and which will cause problems.

## Key Concepts

**Sending Site**  
The facility that originated the process — typically an R&D site, a clinical manufacturing facility, or another commercial site. Responsible for preparing the Tech Transfer Package.

**Receiving Site**  
The facility that will run the process after transfer — typically the commercial manufacturing site. Responsible for demonstrating that the transferred process is reproducible.

**Tech Transfer Package (TTP)**  
The complete set of documents the sending site prepares: process description, batch record, analytical methods, specifications, process characterization data, and more. The TTP is the formal mechanism of knowledge transfer. See: [tech_transfer/tech_transfer_package.md](tech_transfer_package.md)

**Comparability**  
The demonstration that the product made at the receiving site is equivalent in quality, safety, and efficacy to the product made at the sending site. This is required for regulatory purposes. See: [tech_transfer/comparability_studies.md](comparability_studies.md)

## MSAT's Role

MSAT typically leads or co-leads the tech transfer from the receiving site's perspective:

- Reviewing the TTP from the sending site and identifying gaps
- Adapting batch records and SOPs to local equipment and procedures
- Running engineering/demo batches to shake out process issues before formal validation
- Executing the PPQ campaign to formally validate the transferred process
- Writing the comparability report
- Closing out the transfer and handing the process to Manufacturing Operations

On the sending side (less common for commercial MSAT, more common for R&D/Process Development groups), MSAT prepares the TTP and provides technical support to the receiving site throughout the transfer.

---

## Success Criteria

A tech transfer is considered complete when:

1. The receiving site has manufactured a defined number of batches meeting all product specifications
2. The PPQ protocol has been executed and the PPQ report is approved
3. Comparability to the sending site has been demonstrated
4. All regulatory filings are in place (or the site is operating within the existing filing)
5. Manufacturing Operations at the receiving site can run the process independently without MSAT support

See: [tech_transfer/phases.md](phases.md) for what each of these takes in practice.
