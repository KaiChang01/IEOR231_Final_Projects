# Glossary

Key terms used across the wiki. Terms are cross-referenced to the articles where they appear in depth.

---

**API (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient)**  
The biologically active component in a drug product — the molecule that actually has the therapeutic effect. Also called the drug substance. The API is manufactured separately and then formulated into the final dosage form. See: [manufacturing/overview.md](manufacturing/overview.md)

**Batch**  
A specific quantity of a drug produced in one defined cycle according to a single manufacturing order. Batches are the fundamental traceable unit in GMP manufacturing — everything is documented at the batch level. See: [manufacturing/gmp_basics.md](manufacturing/gmp_basics.md)

**Batch Record (or Master Batch Record / Executed Batch Record)**  
The Master Batch Record (MBR) is the approved template of instructions for manufacturing a batch. The Executed Batch Record (EBR) is the filled-in record of what actually happened. GMP requires the EBR to be completed in real time during manufacturing. See: [manufacturing/gmp_basics.md](manufacturing/gmp_basics.md)

**Campaign**  
A series of consecutive batches of the same product produced without interruption or changeover. Campaigns are the key unit of production planning. See: [scheduling/campaign_planning.md](scheduling/campaign_planning.md)

**Change Control**  
The formal process for evaluating, approving, and documenting any proposed change to a validated manufacturing process, equipment, material, or facility. Required by GMP. See: [manufacturing/gmp_basics.md](manufacturing/gmp_basics.md), [regulatory/ich_guidelines.md](regulatory/ich_guidelines.md)

**CMC (Chemistry, Manufacturing and Controls)**  
The section of a regulatory filing (e.g., NDA, BLA) that describes the drug substance, drug product, manufacturing process, controls, and specifications. MSAT is heavily involved in CMC activities. See: [msat/roles_responsibilities.md](msat/roles_responsibilities.md)

**Comparability Study**  
An analytical and sometimes clinical study that demonstrates two versions of a product (e.g., pre- and post-transfer) are equivalent in quality, safety, and efficacy. See: [tech_transfer/comparability_studies.md](tech_transfer/comparability_studies.md)

**CPP (Critical Process Parameter)**  
A process parameter whose variability has a direct impact on a Critical Quality Attribute and therefore must be controlled to ensure the process produces the desired quality. See: [tech_transfer/process_characterization.md](tech_transfer/process_characterization.md)

**CQA (Critical Quality Attribute)**  
A physical, chemical, biological, or microbiological property of the drug product that must be within defined limits to ensure acceptable product quality, safety, and efficacy. The concept comes from ICH Q8. See: [tech_transfer/process_characterization.md](tech_transfer/process_characterization.md), [regulatory/ich_guidelines.md](regulatory/ich_guidelines.md)

**Design Space**  
The multidimensional combination of input variables and process parameters that have been demonstrated to provide assurance of quality. Operating within the design space is not considered a change. Defined in ICH Q8. See: [regulatory/ich_guidelines.md](regulatory/ich_guidelines.md)

**Drug Product**  
The finished dosage form (tablet, vial, syringe, etc.) containing the API plus excipients. Also called the finished product. See: [manufacturing/overview.md](manufacturing/overview.md)

**Drug Substance**  
Another term for the API — the active ingredient before it is formulated into a drug product. See: [manufacturing/overview.md](manufacturing/overview.md)

**EBR (Executed Batch Record)**  
See Batch Record.

**Excipient**  
An inactive ingredient in a drug product — fillers, binders, preservatives, stabilizers, etc. — that is not the API but plays a role in the product's physical form, stability, or delivery. See: [manufacturing/overview.md](manufacturing/overview.md)

**GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice)**  
A system of regulations and practices that ensures pharmaceutical products are consistently produced and controlled to quality standards. In the US, enforced by the FDA via 21 CFR Parts 210/211. Also called cGMP (current GMP). See: [manufacturing/gmp_basics.md](manufacturing/gmp_basics.md)

**ICH (International Council for Harmonisation)**  
An international body that develops globally harmonized guidelines for pharmaceutical development, manufacturing, and quality. Key guidelines for MSAT: Q8, Q9, Q10, Q11. See: [regulatory/ich_guidelines.md](regulatory/ich_guidelines.md)

**IQ / OQ / PQ (Installation, Operational, Performance Qualification)**  
The three-stage qualification protocol for equipment and systems. IQ verifies it was installed correctly; OQ verifies it operates within specified ranges; PQ verifies it performs consistently in actual production conditions. See: [manufacturing/overview.md](manufacturing/overview.md)

**KPP (Key Process Parameter)**  
A process parameter that is important to monitor but whose variability does not directly impact a CQA. Lower priority than CPPs but still tracked. See: [tech_transfer/process_characterization.md](tech_transfer/process_characterization.md)

**MBR (Master Batch Record)**  
See Batch Record.

**MSAT (Manufacturing Science and Technology)**  
The department within a pharmaceutical company responsible for owning the manufacturing process from a scientific and technical standpoint — process development, tech transfer, validation, and ongoing process improvement. See: [msat/overview.md](msat/overview.md)

**NOR / PAR (Normal Operating Range / Proven Acceptable Range)**  
NOR is the range within which a process parameter is routinely controlled. PAR is the broader validated range within which the parameter can be operated without affecting product quality. Established during process characterization. See: [tech_transfer/process_characterization.md](tech_transfer/process_characterization.md)

**OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)**  
A metric for measuring manufacturing equipment efficiency: Availability × Performance × Quality. Used in capacity analysis. See: [scheduling/capacity_modeling.md](scheduling/capacity_modeling.md)

**PPQ (Process Performance Qualification)**  
Stage 2 of process validation per the FDA 2011 guidance — the series of batches that formally demonstrate the commercial manufacturing process is capable and reproducible. See: [tech_transfer/phases.md](tech_transfer/phases.md)

**Process Characterization**  
The scientific study that establishes the relationship between process parameters and product quality attributes. The foundation for defining CPPs, NOR, and PAR. See: [tech_transfer/process_characterization.md](tech_transfer/process_characterization.md)

**Process Validation**  
The collection of evidence demonstrating that a manufacturing process consistently produces a product meeting its predetermined specifications. FDA defines three stages: process design, PPQ, and continued process verification. See: [tech_transfer/phases.md](tech_transfer/phases.md)

**QbD (Quality by Design)**  
A systematic approach to pharmaceutical development that begins with predefined objectives and emphasizes product and process understanding and process control. Defined in ICH Q8. See: [regulatory/ich_guidelines.md](regulatory/ich_guidelines.md)

**Receiving Site**  
In a tech transfer, the facility that will manufacture the product after transfer — typically the commercial manufacturing site. See: [tech_transfer/overview.md](tech_transfer/overview.md)

**Scale-up**  
The process of transferring a manufacturing process from smaller-scale equipment (lab or pilot) to larger commercial-scale equipment while maintaining product quality. See: [tech_transfer/scale_up.md](tech_transfer/scale_up.md)

**Sending Site**  
In a tech transfer, the facility that originated the process and is handing it off — often an R&D site, clinical manufacturing site, or another commercial site. See: [tech_transfer/overview.md](tech_transfer/overview.md)

**SOP (Standard Operating Procedure)**  
A controlled document specifying how to perform a defined task. Required by GMP for all critical manufacturing and quality operations. See: [manufacturing/gmp_basics.md](manufacturing/gmp_basics.md)

**TTP (Tech Transfer Package)**  
The structured collection of documents that the sending site prepares and hands over to the receiving site — contains all process knowledge needed to reproduce the manufacturing process. See: [tech_transfer/tech_transfer_package.md](tech_transfer/tech_transfer_package.md)

**Unit Operation**  
A discrete processing step in a manufacturing process (e.g., granulation, filtration, lyophilization). Complex processes are decomposed into a sequence of unit operations. See: [manufacturing/unit_operations.md](manufacturing/unit_operations.md)

**Validation**  
Documented evidence establishing that a process, system, or piece of equipment consistently produces a result meeting predetermined specifications. Broader than process validation — includes cleaning validation, analytical method validation, equipment qualification, etc. See: [manufacturing/gmp_basics.md](manufacturing/gmp_basics.md)
