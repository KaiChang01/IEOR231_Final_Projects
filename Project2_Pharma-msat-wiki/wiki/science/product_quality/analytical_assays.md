## The Role of Analytics in Manufacturing

Analytical assays are the instruments by which MSAT sees the product. Every conclusion about CQA status, every process characterization study, every comparability assessment, and every batch release decision depends on the data generated by analytical methods. In biopharmaceuticals, you cannot fully characterize the product — but you can characterize it sufficiently, using a suite of orthogonal methods.

The key principle is **orthogonality**: using methods that separate different attributes by different physical principles, so that the blind spots of one method are covered by another. A single method gives a single view; a panel of methods gives a more complete picture.

## Assay Categories

### Potency Assays

The most regulated and challenging category. All potency assays must be validated to demonstrate specificity, accuracy, precision, linearity, and stability-indicating capability.

**Binding assays:**
- **SPR (Surface Plasmon Resonance):** Real-time, label-free binding kinetics. Gold standard for Kd, kon, koff. Instrument: Biacore. Used in characterization; can be formatted for relative potency.
- **ELISA-based binding:** Plate-based assay measuring Fab binding to coated antigen, or Fc binding to FcRn / FcγR. Higher throughput, used for in-process and release.

**Cell-based bioassays:**
- **Cytotoxicity assay (ADCC):** Co-culture of effector cells (NK cells or engineered Jurkat/ADCC reporter cells) with target cells expressing the antigen. Readout: % target cell killing or reporter gene activity.
- **Proliferation inhibition / apoptosis assay:** For mAbs that inhibit growth signaling.
- **Reporter gene assay:** Engineered Jurkat cells with NFAT-luciferase. When the mAb activates FcγRIIIA, the reporter fires. Fast, precise, well-suited for release.

### Purity Assays

**Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC-HPLC):**
- Separates by hydrodynamic radius
- Quantifies: % monomer, % aggregates (HMW species), % fragments (LMW species)
- Standard release assay. Detection by UV A280.
- Limitations: running conditions (mobile phase, column) can affect what aggregates are seen; some small aggregates may co-elute with monomer

**Capillary electrophoresis SDS (CE-SDS):**
- Proteins denatured with SDS, separated by size under electric field in a capillary
- Non-reduced CE-SDS: detects fragments (broken disulfide bonds). Reduced CE-SDS: resolves heavy and light chains, detects extra bands from truncation, glycosylation heterogeneity.
- More quantitative and reproducible than gel-based SDS-PAGE

**Imaged capillary isoelectric focusing (icIEF):**
- Separates by isoelectric point in a pH gradient across a capillary
- Reports: pI of main peak, % acidic variants, % basic variants
- Standard release assay for charge heterogeneity
- Modern icIEF instruments (e.g., iCE3, Maurice) allow imaging of the entire capillary

**Cation exchange HPLC (CEX-HPLC):**
- Chromatographic separation by charge
- Orthogonal to icIEF; different selectivity can separate variants that co-migrate on icIEF
- Also used preparatively: fraction collection for structural identification of individual variants

### Glycosylation Assays

**Released glycan analysis (HILIC-FLD):**
- Glycans released enzymatically with PNGase F, labeled with 2-AB (2-aminobenzamide) or InstantPC fluorescent dye, separated by HILIC (hydrophilic interaction liquid chromatography)
- Quantitative % of each named glycoform: G0F, G1F, G2F, G0, Man5, etc.
- Workhorse release assay for glycan profile

**Intact mass (native MS or denaturing MS):**
- ESI-MS of the intact antibody gives molecular weight spectrum
- At the intact level, resolves broad glycoform envelopes, useful for checking overall composition
- Native MS (non-denaturing conditions) preserves non-covalent interactions and can resolve individual glycoforms with high resolution

**Peptide mapping with glycopeptide enrichment:**
- Site-specific glycosylation analysis — important when multiple glycosylation sites exist
- Measures glycan distribution at each individual site independently

### Identity Assays

**Peptide mapping (LC-MS/MS):**
- Protein digested with proteases (trypsin most common), peptides separated by HPLC, identified by tandem MS
- Confirms amino acid sequence, identifies PTMs (deamidation, oxidation, glycosylation, disulfide bonds)
- Gold standard for primary structure characterization
- Too slow for routine release; used in characterization and comparability

**Intact mass:**
- Exact molecular weight confirms overall amino acid composition (major sequence errors would be detected)
- Rapid QC assay; used in release and in-process

**N-terminal sequencing (Edman degradation):** Largely replaced by MS-based methods but still used for N-terminal characterization.

### Safety / Purity Assays

**HCP ELISA (host cell protein):**
- Measures residual proteins from the CHO production cells
- Multi-analyte ELISA using a polyclonal antibody mixture raised against CHO HCPs
- Expressed in ng HCP per mg drug substance; specification typically <100 ppm (ng/mg)
- Limitation: HCP ELISA coverage is not 100% — some HCPs may not be detected. Orthogonal proteomics approaches (HCP proteomics by LC-MS/MS) are increasingly used for characterization.

**Host cell DNA (qPCR):**
- Quantifies residual CHO genomic DNA
- ICH Q5A guideline: ≤10 ng DNA per dose
- Highly sensitive; detection limit in pg range

**Endotoxin (LAL assay — Limulus amebocyte lysate):**
- Detects gram-negative bacterial endotoxin (lipopolysaccharide)
- Clot-based, turbidimetric, or chromogenic formats
- Specification: typically <0.1–1 EU/mg or per dose (depends on route of administration)

**Sterility (USP <71>):**
- Membrane filtration or direct inoculation of the drug product into culture media
- Incubated for 14 days; presence of growth = failure
- Very slow; rapid sterility methods (ATP bioluminescence, flow cytometry) are available but require regulatory acceptance

**Subvisible particles (USP <788> / <787>):**
- Light obscuration: counts particles ≥10 µm and ≥25 µm (compendial release specification)
- Micro-flow imaging (MFI): counts and images particles ≥1–2 µm; distinguishes protein particles from silicone oil droplets and other contaminants

## Assay Validation

All assays used for release or CQA monitoring must be validated per ICH Q2(R1) (analytical procedure validation). Key parameters:

| Parameter | Definition | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity | Assay measures the intended analyte without interference | All assays |
| Accuracy | Closeness of result to true value | Quantitative assays |
| Precision (repeatability / intermediate) | Closeness of results within and between runs | All quantitative assays |
| Linearity | Linear response over stated range | Quantitative assays |
| Range | Interval over which assay is accurate, precise, linear | Quantitative assays |
| Detection limit (LOD) | Smallest detectable amount | Impurity assays |
| Quantitation limit (LOQ) | Smallest reliably quantified amount | Impurity assays |
| Robustness | Insensitivity to small deliberate variations | All assays |

**Stability-indicating validation** — for assays used in stability studies, additional experiments demonstrate that the assay can detect product degradation (e.g., forced degradation samples show detectable changes in the assay).

## Reference Standards

Every quantitative assay uses a reference standard — a well-characterized lot of the drug substance against which test samples are compared. The reference standard anchors the potency unit and provides the expected analytical profile.

Reference standard management is a critical MSAT function:
- Primary reference standard: highly characterized, stored in small aliquots, not routinely used
- Working reference standard: qualified against primary; used in day-to-day assays
- Reference standard replacement: qualification study when a new lot is needed (bridge study comparing old and new lots)
